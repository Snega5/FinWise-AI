# Investing Basics

## Save first, invest second
Before investing, build an emergency fund and clear high-interest debt such as credit card balances. Investments can lose value in the short term, so money you may need within a few years generally belongs in safer, easily accessible savings.

## Time horizon and risk
Your time horizon is how long you can leave money untouched. Longer horizons (five to ten years or more) can usually tolerate more ups and downs, because markets have more time to recover. Higher potential returns come with higher risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to see fluctuate.

## Diversification
Diversification means spreading money across different assets, sectors and regions so that one poor performer does not damage your whole portfolio. Broad, low-cost index funds are a common way for beginners to diversify without picking individual stocks.

## Regular investing (SIPs)
Investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule, such as a monthly systematic investment plan (SIP), builds the habit and spreads your purchases over time. This reduces the impact of buying everything at a market peak. It does not remove risk or guarantee profit.

## Costs matter
Fees compound just like returns. Compare expense ratios and avoid products you do not understand. Be cautious of anything that promises guaranteed high returns.
