# Emergency Fund and Savings

## Why an emergency fund matters
An emergency fund is money set aside for unexpected costs such as a job loss, a medical bill or an urgent repair. Having it prevents you from borrowing at high interest when something goes wrong.

## How much to save
A common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses. People with irregular income or dependents often aim for the higher end. A smaller first milestone, such as one month of expenses, makes the goal feel reachable.

## Where to keep it
Keep an emergency fund in a safe, easily accessible place such as a savings account. Safety and quick access matter more than high returns for this money.

## Pay yourself first
Move a fixed amount to savings as soon as income arrives, before spending on anything else. Automating the transfer removes the need for willpower. Even a small regular amount builds the habit.

## Savings goals
Give each goal a name, a target amount and a timeline. Divide the remaining amount by the months available to find the monthly contribution. Separate goals, such as a trip, a gadget or a course, are easier to stay committed to than one general pool.

## Sinking funds
A sinking fund is a small amount saved each month for a known future expense, such as annual insurance or festival spending. It turns large irregular bills into small predictable ones.
