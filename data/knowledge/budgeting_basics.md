# Budgeting Basics

## What a budget is
A budget is a plan for your money. It lists what you expect to earn each month and decides in advance where it will go, so spending follows your priorities instead of happening by accident.

## The 50/30/20 rule
The 50/30/20 rule is a simple starting framework. About 50% of after-tax income goes to needs such as rent, utilities, groceries, transport and minimum debt payments. About 30% goes to wants such as dining out, shopping, entertainment and subscriptions. The remaining 20% goes to savings and extra debt repayment. The percentages are a guide, not a law. In high-cost cities needs may take more than half, and the split should be adjusted to your situation.

## Zero-based budgeting
In a zero-based budget, every unit of income is assigned a job until income minus planned spending and saving equals zero. It does not mean spending everything; savings are one of the jobs. This method works well for people who want tight control.

## Track before you cut
Review two or three months of real transactions before setting limits. Averages by category show where money actually goes. Set budgets slightly below the average for flexible categories such as shopping and entertainment, and keep fixed categories such as rent unchanged.

## Review monthly
Compare actual spending with the budget once a month. Adjust limits that were unrealistic, and note one-off events such as annual fees or repairs so they do not distort the picture.
