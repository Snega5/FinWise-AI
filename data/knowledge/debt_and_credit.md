# Debt and Credit Basics

## Good debt and costly debt
Debt that finances something likely to grow in value or earning power, such as education, is generally easier to justify than debt for consumption. Credit card balances and some personal loans carry high interest, so they deserve priority repayment.

## Paying down debt
Two popular methods are the avalanche and the snowball. With the avalanche method you pay minimums on everything and send extra money to the debt with the highest interest rate first, which minimises total interest. With the snowball method you target the smallest balance first, which builds motivation through quick wins. Either works if you stay consistent.

## Credit utilisation
Credit utilisation is the share of your credit limit that you are using. Keeping it low, often suggested below 30%, tends to support a healthier credit score. Paying the full statement balance each month avoids interest altogether.

## Credit score basics
A credit score reflects repayment history, how much credit you use, the length of your credit history and how often you apply for new credit. Paying on time is the single most important habit. Setting up automatic payments for at least the minimum due helps avoid missed dates.

## EMIs
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both principal and interest on a loan. Before taking one, check that the total of all EMIs stays comfortably within your monthly budget, and compare the total interest cost, not just the monthly amount.
