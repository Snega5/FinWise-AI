# Smart Spending Habits

## Reduce impulse purchases
Add a waiting period of 24 to 48 hours before buying non-essential items. Many urges fade, and what remains is more likely to be a real need. Unsubscribing from promotional emails and removing saved card details from shopping apps adds useful friction.

## Food spending
Food is often the most flexible large category. Planning meals for the week, buying groceries from a list and limiting delivery orders can reduce spending without reducing quality. Cooking in batches saves both money and time.

## Subscriptions
List every recurring charge and ask whether you used each one in the last month. Cancel unused services, share family plans where the terms allow, and rotate streaming services instead of paying for all of them at once.

## Transport
Combine errands into fewer trips, use public transport or shared rides for routine travel, and keep vehicles maintained to avoid costly repairs and poor fuel economy.

## Spot unusual spending
A single large purchase can distort a month. Check whether a spike is a one-off, such as a planned purchase, or a growing habit. Reviewing your largest transactions each month is a quick way to catch surprises, duplicate charges and forgotten subscriptions.

## Needs versus wants
Before buying, ask whether the item is a need, a want you can afford, or a want you cannot afford right now. Being honest about that third group is where most savings are found.
